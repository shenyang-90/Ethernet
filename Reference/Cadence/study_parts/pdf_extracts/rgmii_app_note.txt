RGMII clocking
The RGMII standard specifies a source synchronous clock. In the egress direction, the data and clock are
transmitted from the SoC chip containing the GEM GXL to the RGMII PHY chip. In the ingress direction,
the data and clock are transmitted from the RGMII PHY chip to the SoC chip containing the GEM GXL. The
data is sampled at the receiving end using the same edge of the clock on which it was generated. To do
this safely, the clock must have a longer path delay than the data. The RGMII standard supports two
different versions. Version 1.3 relies on a PCB trace delay of 1.5 to 2.0 ns for the clock, whereas version
2.0 and later offers the option of introducing the delay on-chip at the source. Devices which support the
internal delay (ID) are referred to as RGMII-ID. The GEM GXL does not have any clock generation logic
within the IP and therefore it is the responsibility of the implementer to drive the tx_clk transmit clock
output from the SoC appropriately.
Most RGMII PHY chips will provide support for delaying the input clock and output clock. If this is available,
the integrator may decide to not implement any egress clock delays (either inside the SoC, or on the PCB
trace). The GEM GXL IP supports both versions of the RGMII spec but it is the responsibility of the
implementer to insert the appropriate delay to support RGMII-ID if it is needed. A buffer with programmable
delay could for example be used to drive the delayed tx_clk signal going to the RGMII PHY.
The other main difference between version 1.3 and 2.0 is the type of pads used. Version 2.0 utilizes 1.5 V
HSTL pads, whereas version 1.3 uses 2.5 V CMOS pads.

It can be assumed that at the receiving end within the SoC containing the GEM GXL IP, the clock from the
RGMII PHY will already be delayed with respect to the data (centre-aligned). From the RGMII standard,
this delay must be at minimum 1ns at the receiving end. There is also a hold requirement of at least 1ns,
so this infers the delay between clock and data will always be between 1ns and 3ns. We assume that
differences between the clock/data trace delay, pad delays, pin capacitance, and rise/fall time are negligible.
Within the SoC, we must ensure that the delays from rgmii_rxd/rgmii_rx_ctl input and the generated
rx_clk/n_rx_clk clocks to the first flops are constrained appropriately to ensure the data can be
sampled safely – a difference of more than 1ns will cause a problem.

RGMII Transmit
For RGMII operation, the system clock controller is required to provide the tx_clk source and the inverted
n_tx_clk source to the IP, and the external tx_clk output for the PHY. To ensure that the skew
requirements of the RGMII are met, for RGMII version 1.3 the delays on this external clock output should
be matched to the RGMII transmit data and control outputs (edge-aligned), for RGMII-ID the transmit clock
output should be delayed 2ns relative to these outputs (centre-aligned).

The lower nibble of the data byte rgmii_txd[3:0] is available on the rising edge of tx_clk and the
upper nibble is available on the following falling edge of tx_clk. The signal tx_clk_sig selects the lower
nibble to be output on rgmii_txd when high and the upper nibble when low.
tx_clk_sig is an input to the GEM_GXL used to control the multiplexer logic feeding the DDR outputs. It
is the responsibility of the system integrator to produce this signal, but it can be sourced from tx_clk.
Since the main RGMII outputs directly feed chip level pads, it is generally recommended to avoid glitches
on these signals. In the diagram above, tx_clk_sig is delayed by approximately 2ns (a 90 degree shift
of tx_clk). It does not necessarily have to be set to this value, but delaying the mux select by a sufficient
amount ensures the relevant inputs to that mux are stable when tx_clk_sig changes. Note that ensuring
the mux outputs are glitch-free is not an RGMII specific timing requirement bus is considered a sensible
approach. Two examples that the integrator may choose for generating tx_clk_sig are shown below:

The delay shown in timing path C should be greater than both timing path A and B. STA of the 3 paths
needs to include the clock tree delays and should be performed across all corners.
In terms of synthesis constraints, rgmii_txd and rgmii_tx_ctl are top level outputs and the paths from
final flop to output should be tightly constrained to avoid major routing or cell delays being added. There is
some margin allowed within the standard (up to 1ns drift at the receiving end) but it is recommended that
the path from the tx_clk and n_tx_clk sources through the flop and mux to the outputs be constrained
tightly. The RGMII standard states the variation should be within 500ps at the transmitting end before any
delay to offset the clock/data is added. Similarly, the path from tx_clk_sig through to the outputs should
be constrained similarly tightly.

Note that from release 1p09 onwards, when RGMII is configured the core supports operating in MII mode
by selecting “sel_mii_on_rgmii”, bit 28 of the network control register, offset 0x0. When this is active
gmii_txd in the Transmit RGMII diagram above is replaced with {txd[3:0], txd[3:0]} to make the
upper and lower nibbles identical. rgmii_txd becomes the MII txd output, rgmii_tx_ctl becomes
the MII tx_en and a delayed version of gmii_tx_er is output at the top-level to create the MII tx_er
output. tx_clk will be sourced from the PHY in MII mode, but the clock trees will be identical and no
specific synthesis constraints considerations are needed to support this mode.
